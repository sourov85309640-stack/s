Roofing advice.

Short, honest guides for homeowners. No ladders needed for any of them.

  • Checking your roof from the ground 3 minute read

    Stand back on the pavement or at the end of the garden, ideally with binoculars, on a bright day.

    • Look for gaps or lines of tiles that are not straight.
    • Check the ridge for missing or loose ridge tiles.
    • Look at the lead and pointing where the roof meets the chimney.
    • Follow the gutters: sagging, plants growing or water stains below a joint all mean a blockage or a leak.

    Take photos as you go. They help us more than you might think.

  • After a storm: what to look for 2 minute read

    Wait until the wind has dropped. Then, from the ground:

    • Look for tiles, slates or ridge pieces on the ground or in the gutter.
    • Check the loft with a torch for daylight or wet patches.
    • Photograph any damage for your insurer before anything is moved.
    • If water is coming in, put a bucket under it, move electrics away and call us.
  • Moss on the roof: when it matters 2 minute read

    A little moss is mostly cosmetic. It starts to matter when:

    • It is thick enough to lift the edges of tiles.
    • It washes down and blocks gutters and downpipes.
    • It sits in valleys and holds water against the lead.

    Avoid pressure washing: it can strip the surface off older tiles and force water underneath. Gentle hand removal and a treatment last longer.
