Roofs we work on.

Every roof is built a little differently. Pick yours to see how it is laid and what usually goes wrong with it.

Clay and concrete tiles

The most common roof in the area. Each tile hangs on a timber batten by its nibs, and every few courses are nailed.

  • Usually goes wrong: slipped or cracked tiles, a loose ridge, perished mortar at the verges.
  • What we do: match and replace tiles, re-bed or dry-fix ridges, re-tile a slope or the whole roof.

Natural slate

Thin split slates, each held by two nails or a hook, laid so every joint is covered by the slate above.

  • Usually goes wrong: nails rust through and slates slide down, called nail sickness, and old slates crack.
  • What we do: refix slipped slates with tingles or hooks, replace with matched slate, re-slate when too many have gone.
Ask about a slate roof

Stone slate

Heavy limestone slates laid in diminishing courses: the biggest at the eaves, getting smaller towards the ridge.

  • Usually goes wrong: oak pegs rot, battens give way, moss holds water, slates slip in groups.
  • What we do: sort, re-peg and re-lay sound slates, top up with reclaimed stone of the same size and colour.
Ask about a stone slate roof

Flat roofs

Extensions, garages and dormers, covered in felt, rubber (EPDM) or fibreglass (GRP) over a timber deck.

  • Usually goes wrong: ponding water, splits at the edges, blisters, failed upstands against a wall.
  • What we do: patch where it is sound, or strip and re-cover with a new deck and better falls.
Ask about a flat roof

Lead and metal

Flashings, valleys, bays and porches, dressed by hand so water runs past every join.

  • Usually goes wrong: splits from pieces that were too long, lifted flashings, theft, old soldered patches.
  • What we do: renew lead in the right sizes and codes, re-dress and re-point flashings, line valleys and gutters.
Ask about leadwork

Older and listed roofs

Handmade clay, old peg tiles and lime mortar. Repairs keep as much of the old roof as possible.

  • Usually goes wrong: soft handmade tiles flake, lime pointing washes out, hidden timbers decay.
  • What we do: reuse what is sound, source matching reclaimed materials, and work with your conservation officer where needed.
